    The parts of the system

    The previous chapter explained why nolainocr is split the way it is. This chapter is a map of what sits on each side of that split: the website, the extraction engine, identity, and settlement.

    If you have not read How nolainocr is built, start there. The words page, session, schema, template group, and page quota are used here with the meanings that chapter introduced.

    This chapter defines the running components and their boundaries. The next chapter owns the job-by-job walkthrough.

    One browser, one service

    When you extract a stack of invoices, your browser talks to a hosted service. Nothing in this public book is a local engine you install. The product is the website and the work it starts on your behalf.

    The website is what you see: landing and solutions pages, sign-in, dashboard, account, blog, and free tools. It does not perform OCR in the browser for the paid extraction path. It uploads pages, shows previews and progress, and renders the table.

    The extraction engine is the hosted worker that reads pages. It is the only part that sends document pixels to a vision model, aligns columns, and returns structured rows. Multi-page jobs run in the background so a long stack does not depend on a single request staying open.

    Around that engine sit four supporting roles:

    • A job queue — holds work and progress so the website can show a bar instead of spinning on one HTTP call.
    • Identity — who you are, whether email is verified, whether the session is valid.
    • Account records — plan, usage, top-up balance.
    • A payment provider (Stripe) — checkout, customer portal, and subscription events. Card data stays there.

    A health check exists for operators. Users see success as a filled results table, not as an infrastructure dashboard.

    The free tools at nolainocr.com/tools stay on the experience layer and never enter orchestration. That is why they do not consume pages.

    What the extraction engine produces

    The primary result is always a table: named columns and values ready for Excel, CSV, JSON, or Google Sheets. Everything else is supporting machinery.

    Stage Consumes Produces User-visible?
    Accept file PDF or images Session pages and previews Upload panel
    Learn schema First page Field names for the batch Implicit in column headers
    Read remaining pages Later pages + schema Per-page records Progress on large jobs
    Align and flatten Nested or variant labels Rectangular columns The results grid
    Settle pages Completed page count Updated quota Account usage

    Optional artifacts such as a saved “project library” are not part of the current product. The dashboard works on the session in front of you; export is how results leave.

    Trust and failure boundaries

    • Transport. The site is served over HTTPS in production.
    • Documents. Visible to the extraction engine for the session; deleted on logout.
    • Accounts. Email/password or Google. Protected screens require a verified email.
    • Payments. Stripe checkout and portal; nolainocr records plan state, not PAN/CVC.
    • Capacity. If monthly quota and top-ups are empty, orchestration refuses the job rather than running extraction for free.
    • Layout. Mixed templates fail as poor tables, not as a silent rewrite into a different product.

    When the vision provider or the job queue is unavailable, large extractions fail in the dashboard. Retry once, then contact with the time and the visible error. Internal recovery is a maintainer concern.
